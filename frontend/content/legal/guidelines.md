## In plain words

JACOB is a quiet place for your small group to stay close between
Sundays. The point of these guidelines is to keep this space worthy of
that — kind, honest, safe, and rooted in respect for the people you
share it with. Read them once when you join. Come back to them if
something feels off. If you ever see something that breaks them, use
the report flow inside the app.

## The spirit of this place

This is a Christian small-group app, built first for a Christian Jiu
Jitsu organization. The way we treat each other here should reflect that —
warmly, without performance, the way you'd want to be treated by
someone who knows your name.

You don't have to be eloquent. You don't have to be on your best
spiritual behavior. Bring your real life. We just ask that you bring
it with care for the people on the other side of the screen.

## Treat each other with respect

- **Speak to people, not at them.** Members of your group are real
  people with names, kids, jobs, hard weeks. Write the way you'd
  speak to them in person.
- **Disagree without contempt.** People will see things differently,
  even within a small group. Push back on the idea, not the person.
- **Assume good faith.** Read the most charitable plausible meaning
  of someone's words before you respond.
- **Apologize when you miss.** A short, real apology repairs more
  than a long defense ever will.

## What's not allowed

The following are not welcome in JACOB and may result in content
removal, account suspension, or termination, depending on severity:

- **Harassment, bullying, or personal attacks.** This includes pile-ons,
  doxxing, and persistent unwanted contact after someone has asked you
  to stop.
- **Hate speech.** Slurs, dehumanizing language, or content that
  attacks people because of their race, ethnicity, national origin,
  religion, gender, sexual orientation, disability, or age.
- **Threats and incitement to violence.** Including credible threats,
  glorification of violence, and content meant to recruit others into
  violent action.
- **Sexual content involving minors.** This is a hard line. Any image
  or text we identify as child sexual abuse material is reported to
  the National Center for Missing & Exploited Children (NCMEC) and to
  law enforcement, as we are required to do by U.S. law. The account
  is removed immediately.
- **Adult sexual content.** JACOB is a small-group app and
  is not the place for it.
- **Violent or graphic imagery,** including self-harm content posted
  to encourage rather than to seek help.
- **Spam.** Unsolicited bulk messaging, repetitive promotional posts,
  scams, phishing, or malware.
- **Commercial solicitation** that the group hasn't asked for.
  Mentioning your business when it's relevant is fine; turning a
  small group into a sales channel is not.
- **Impersonation.** Pretending to be another person or organization
  in a way meant to deceive.
- **Privacy violations.** Posting someone else's private information
  (address, phone, photos shared in confidence) without their consent.
- **Illegal activity.** Anything that would be illegal in the
  jurisdiction where it took place, including coordinating it.

## A note on faith conversations

JACOB is a Christian app and faith conversations are part of why it
exists. Inside your own group, talk freely about Scripture, prayer,
doubt, what you're learning. That's the whole point.

A few things to hold lightly so this stays a place people want to be:

- **Inside your group, give each other room.** Believers come from
  many traditions and seasons. Pressing someone to adopt your specific
  tradition, denomination, or theological position can feel less like
  brotherhood and more like a sales pitch. Share your conviction, then
  let it sit.
- **Across faith lines, lead with respect.** If a member of your group
  is from a different faith background — or none — the goal is
  friendship and honest conversation, not conversion as a project. The
  Bible itself models invitation, not pressure.
- **No proselytizing across groups you weren't invited to.** Don't
  cold-message people in groups you haven't been invited into for the
  purpose of preaching at them. That's not the kind of witness this
  app is for.

These are guidelines for *how* faith shows up in this space, not a
softening of the gospel. Bring your faith. Bring it the way Christ
modeled — patient, particular, kind.

## Photos and uploads

- Only post photos you have the right to share.
- Don't post photos of minors who aren't your own children, or whose
  parents or guardians haven't given permission.
- Don't post sexual or graphically violent imagery.
- All photo uploads are screened by an automated safety system before
  they appear in a group. False positives can happen; if your image
  is wrongly held back, use the appeals link on the rejection notice.

## When something goes wrong

If you see content that breaks these guidelines, you have a few
options:

- **Mute or block** another member to stop seeing them or hearing from
  them. Use these any time, no questions asked.
- **Report** a message, post, or account to the moderation team. The
  Report link is on every message and on the member's profile page.
  Reports go to our moderators, not back to the reported user.
- **Leave the group.** If the group itself isn't a place you want to be
  anymore, you can leave at any time. If a leader is the problem,
  report the leader as well.

A moderator will review reported content and decide whether to remove
it, warn the user, mute them, suspend them, or — for severe or
repeated violations — terminate the account. We aim to act on reports
within 48 hours.

## Consequences and appeals

When we take action against an account or piece of content, we tell
the user what happened and why. The user can appeal, and the appeal is
reviewed by a different human moderator than the one who made the
original decision. We aim to respond to appeals within 14 days.

Some violations — possession or distribution of CSAM, credible threats
of violence, attacks on the platform itself — result in immediate
permanent removal without the standard appeals process.

## How leaders can help

If you lead a group, you set the tone more than any guideline does.
A few small habits help:

- **Welcome new members by name.** A short, real greeting on day one
  makes the next message easier to write.
- **Model honesty and curiosity.** When you ask a real question, the
  group learns it's a place where real questions are welcome.
- **Address problems quietly and quickly.** A short DM ("hey, that one
  landed sharper than I think you meant — can you take another pass?")
  works far better than a public callout.
- **Use mute, not exile, where you can.** Muting a noisy member for a
  day is often enough; banning them is rarely the right first step.
